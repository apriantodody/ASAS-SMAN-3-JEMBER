Text 1 – The Forest Was Crying

In a small village near a tropical forest in Kalimantan, a teenage boy named Raka loved exploring nature. He often played near the river and watched birds flying above the trees.

One dry afternoon, Raka noticed thick smoke rising from the forest. A large area of land was burning. Some people had deliberately cleared the land by setting fires to prepare plantations. Raka immediately informed the village leader, but some residents believed that burning the land was a cheap and easy way to prepare agricultural areas.

Days passed, and the fire spread further. The smoke covered the village, making it difficult for people to breathe. Schools had to stop outdoor activities, and several animals lost their natural habitats.

When the rainy season arrived, another problem appeared. Heavy rain caused flooding in some areas where the forest had been damaged. The villagers realized that destroying forests could create serious environmental problems.

Raka then encouraged his friends to organize an environmental campaign. They educated residents about the dangers of land burning and the importance of protecting forests.

Text 2 – Forest Fires and Floods Remain a Challenge

JAKARTA – November 9, 2026

Forest and land fires (Karhutla) continue to threaten several regions in Indonesia. According to the National Disaster Mitigation Agency (BNPB), fires were reported in Gowa, South Sulawesi, and Mamasa, West Sulawesi, in early November 2026.

In Gowa, approximately one hectare of land was burned, while a fire in Mamasa affected around three hectares. Local disaster management teams, firefighters, police officers, and residents worked together to extinguish the fires. Both fires were successfully put out.

BNPB also reported that Indonesia had recorded 2,328 disaster events by November 9, 2026. Many of these disasters were related to weather and environmental conditions.

As the rainy season approaches, BNPB has warned that areas previously affected by forest fires may face additional risks, including floods and landslides. Damaged forests have a reduced ability to absorb rainwater and protect the soil.

BNPB has encouraged communities to stop illegal land burning, report fire incidents immediately, clean drainage systems, and follow official weather updates.

Adapted from BNPB reports, November 9, 2026.

Text 3 – QRIS: Making Digital Payments Easier

Digital payments are becoming increasingly popular in Indonesia. Through QRIS (Quick Response Code Indonesian Standard), people can pay for food, transportation, and shopping simply by scanning a QR code.

There are several reasons why digital payments are beneficial. First, they make transactions faster and more convenient. Second, small businesses can accept payments without preparing large amounts of cash. Third, users can check their transaction history to monitor their spending.

The development of QRIS in Indonesia has shown significant growth. In Banten, for example, QRIS transactions reached Rp101.1 trillion from January to September 2026, with approximately 1.2 billion transactions. Most QRIS merchants in the province were small and medium-sized businesses (UMKM).

However, digital payments also have disadvantages. Internet disruptions may prevent transactions, while online fraud can put users' money at risk. Some people may also spend excessively because digital payments make spending less noticeable.

Therefore, digital payments should be used wisely. Users need to understand financial literacy, protect their personal information, and check every transaction carefully.
